<Chapter n="01" title="Context">

The Finnish dating service, Treffit24, has been around for almost 2 decades, and it feels like it. As it exists only as a web application, the client wanted us to extend it into a modern mobile application, to help reach the business goals of:

- reaching a new audience via the App and Play Stores
- attracting more women to the service
- increasing premium sales conversions

<Callout>  How might we increase love-seekers engagement to find a significant other - and eventually delete their profile?</Callout>


</Chapter>

### Service blueprint

With the help of my whole team: business, development, marketing, sales, and analytics, I ideated a service blueprint.  
From the first version, I iterated and detailed it during the following months, and made it visible to our stakeholders by stapling it to a wall. Tape was too weak for such a large and heavy document.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/TREFFIT-service-blueprint.jpg"
  alt={`The first service blueprint for the Treffit app, hand-drawn across two pages of a dotted notebook. Rows for time, evidence, user actions, onstage actions, backstage actions, support processes and metrics. The user’s journey runs left to right: hear about the service (“a friend told me to check it”), consider using it, join, explore (“I could find someone here”), evaluate, then engage with other users and dates over days or months, commit and buy, and finally exit (“I found the one”). Underneath, what the service does at each step, such as stories, automated search, spam filtering, block and report, date suggestions and profile curation, and a metric for each stage, ending with the share of leaving users who found love on Treffit.`}

<Chapter n="06" title="Learnings">

### Anti-personas

I briefly worked on personas - mainly from a marketing approach. I realised that it was useful for us to also build personas of users we wanted to get rid of (the pervs, the stalkers, the scammers), to design a user experience that would turn them off.

### Project management

We had good team dynamics, yet project management was difficult as we had 2 to 4 developers working simultaneously on at least 3 products at the same time, all the time. I worked closely with my Product Owner to try different methods and tools.


</Chapter>
