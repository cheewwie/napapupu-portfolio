<Chapter n="01" title="Context">

The International Ski Federation (FIS) reached out to us to redesign their app from scratch, with the goals of:

- exiting their previous vendor lock
- modernising the UI
- improving the functionality

<Callout>How might we leverage the new app to improve the end-to-end FIS user experience and their retention?</Callout>


</Chapter>

<Chapter
  n="02"
  title="Discover"

>

### Concept kickoff
I organised and facilitated a workshop with the client stakeholders, some of our engineers and my designer pair.  
We covered the basics: goals, target audience, and prioritising the ideas within the agreed scope, with the flexibility to update scope and priorities later.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/FIS-workshop-outcome.png"
  alt={``}
  caption="OUTCOME OF THE STAKEHOLDERS WORKSHOP — from there we understood better what to do, for whom, and why"
/>

### Business insights

- New target audience is younger and more casually following snow sports, wants more media content and follows more than one discipline
- New app needs to support the old features for the existing audience, and needs data and stats in real time
- Having 2 separate apps is not an option

### Technical insights

- Get data from one backend, media content from another backend (CMS), that both also will feed the new website

</Chapter>

### Information architecture

We aimed to enable fans to follow more than one discipline, without having to constantly swap back and forth.  
For that we needed a dynamic structure where all the content would be filtered according to the fan’s preferences. I came up with the “interconnected” concept where different types of concept can be found via different routes.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/FIS-interconnected-IA.png"
  alt={``}
  caption="INTERCONNECTED INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE — an app where you can navigate vertically and horizontally"
/>

With my design pair, we wireframed all the main parts of the new app to get an understanding of the user flows and navigation.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/FIS-wireframes.png"
  alt={``}
  caption="WIREFRAMES — some of our initial sketches of the main sections of the new app"
/>

### Backend structure

We needed to work with two backends (one of which we were building anew) and both backends needed to work with the new website we were building in parallel. I also supported their designer when needed, so that we would produce a coherent system.
I outlined the different types, categories and other parameters for the media content, so that all four parts would work together. That's how I discovered we needed a matrix architecture, not a tree.

<Artefact

### Refining the details

From the wireframes, we refined and detailed our sketches. I focused more on interactions and UX while my design pair focused on refining visuals and art direction.  
We used the existing brand book as a guide, but were given complete freedom to modernise the brand identity (except the logo).  
Our team had then ready-to-develop mockups to work with.

<Chapter n="05" title="Results">

### Fans and athletes feedback

We tested our concept via a prototype and, once it was available, a TestFlight app. The overall feedback was very positive, but also helped us make the necessary changes.

<ResearchQuotes>
  <ResearchQuote source="Cross-country skiing fan" method="Interview" year={2023}>It would be awesome to watch official FIS livestreams in the app.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Former alpine skiing athlete" method="Interview" year={2023}>It's nice to filter out other disciplines and concentrate on a certain one. A personalized feed sounds like a really nice feature.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Snowboard fan and former coach" method="Interview" year={2023}>Before the start list is available, the details give me nothing. Seeing that there will be a start list would be more fulfilling.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Cross-country skiing fan" method="Interview" year={2023}>When onsite for lower level comps I want the live results!!</ResearchQuote>
</ResearchQuotes>

We also got very positive feedback from athletes at competitions, via our stakeholders.

<Pullquote attribution="Marketing Manager · FIS" >
It’s really cool and big step forward and your design mock up’s in the first place played a big role that we came together to deliver this new app in the first place!
</Pullquote>

The project has since passed on to another team, but last time I checked it was still carrying the same vision that we designed! Check it out on [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fis_ski.mobile_fis_ski&pli=1) or [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fis-official-ski-snowboard/id1161730142).

Reaktor has also written a [case study](https://www.reaktor.com/case-studies/fis/) about the project, which you can read for more details.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="06" title="Learnings">

### People matter

I already knew that, but it showed particularly in this project. While not the most challenging in substance, it wasn't without its challenges. Having a whole team supporting me, and me supporting them, is my most precious takeaway from this project.

<Pullquote attribution="Fullstack Developer · Reaktor" >
Work-wise I've really enjoyed her design-work! I feel like Alice was one of the key factors that made the project enjoyable.
</Pullquote>

<Pullquote attribution="Mobile Developer • Reaktor" >
One reason I joined Reaktor in the first place was to have that kind of collaboration with the designers.
</Pullquote>

<Pullquote attribution="Delivery Lead · Reaktor" >
Thank you Alice for all your work, help and laughs!
</Pullquote>

### Choosing my battles

Running a project from scratch without notice of the absence of a project manager is not the easiest thing. We had to improvise, one of us took the hat and we did our best to support them. I took a little too much on myself at times, and didn’t have anymore the bandwidth to be a designer – and burnt myself out a little.

</Chapter>
