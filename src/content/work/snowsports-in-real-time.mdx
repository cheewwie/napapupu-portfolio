<Chapter n="01" title="Context">

The International Ski Federation (FIS) asked us to redesign their app from scratch, to:

- get out of their previous vendor lock-in
- modernise the UI
- improve the functionality

<Callout>How might we leverage the new app to improve the end-to-end FIS user experience and their retention?</Callout>

### Concept kickoff
I organised and facilitated a workshop with the client's stakeholders, some of our engineers and my design pair.  
We covered goals, target audience and priorities within the agreed scope, knowing both could change later.

  src="/case-assets/FIS-workshop-outcome.png"

### Business insights

- The new audience is younger, follows snow sports more casually and across several disciplines, and wants more media content
- The existing audience still needs the old features, plus data and stats in real time
- Two separate apps are not an option

### Technical insights

- Data comes from one backend and media from another (a CMS), and both also feed the new website

### Information architecture

Fans should be able to follow more than one discipline without constantly switching back and forth.  
That needed a dynamic structure, with all content filtered by the fan’s preferences. I came up with the “interconnected” concept, where each type of content can be reached by more than one route.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/FIS-interconnected-IA.png"
  alt={`The interconnected information architecture, with the principle “Whatever path the fan can imagine taking to access content and knowledge, that path is available.” Three columns. Content, split into data (calendar and schedule, results, live ticker, standings) and media (short format such as stories, long format such as articles and replays, livestream). Micro landing pages, where the fan can follow a specific athlete or calendar event, with Disciplines marked in pink as an open question. ### Backend structure

The app had to work with two backends, one of them brand new, and both also fed a new website being built in parallel. I supported the website’s designer when needed, to keep the whole system coherent.
I mapped the types, categories and other parameters of the media content so that all four parts would fit together. That's how I found out we needed a matrix architecture, not a tree.

<Artefact
  src="/case-assets/FIS-backend-architecture.png"
  alt={`The media parameters, with pink marking what was still open. Four columns. Content items: article, image, video and in-app story from Storyblok, and calendar event, athlete page and standing from the FIS database, with the question “How do we deal with those?”. Categories: highlight, livestream, recap, interview. Levels: Olympic Winter Games, World Cup, World Ski Championships, Europa Cup, Nor-Am Cup and more. Disciplines: cross-country, jump, combined, alpine, freeski and more. Two open questions underneath. On structure: naming conventions, a hierarchy or levels, sub-categories as subjects and categories as disciplines, and “Matrix, not tree: is that a possible way to do it?”. On metadata: what metadata do we need?`}

### Refining the details

We then turned the wireframes into detailed designs. I focused on interactions and UX, my design pair on visuals and art direction.  
The existing brand book was our guide, but we had complete freedom to modernise the brand identity (except the logo).  
The result: ready-to-develop mockups for the team.

### Fans and athletes feedback

We tested the concept with a prototype, then with a TestFlight app. Feedback was very positive, and it also showed us what needed changing.

<ResearchQuotes>
  <ResearchQuote source="Cross-country skiing fan" method="Interview" year={2023}>It would be awesome to watch official FIS livestreams in the app.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Former alpine skiing athlete" method="Interview" year={2023}>It's nice to filter out other disciplines and concentrate on a certain one. A personalized feed sounds like a really nice feature.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Snowboard fan and former coach" method="Interview" year={2023}>Before the start list is available, the details give me nothing. Seeing that there will be a start list would be more fulfilling.</ResearchQuote>
  <ResearchQuote source="Cross-country skiing fan" method="Interview" year={2023}>When onsite for lower level comps I want the live results!!</ResearchQuote>
</ResearchQuotes>

Our stakeholders also passed on very positive feedback from athletes at competitions.

### Choosing my battles

Starting a project from scratch and finding out there’s no project manager isn’t easy. We improvised: one of us took on the role, and the rest of us supported them. At times I took on too much, ran out of bandwidth for design work, and burnt out a little.
